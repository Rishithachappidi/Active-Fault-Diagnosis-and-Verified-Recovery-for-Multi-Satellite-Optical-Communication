Full results

MATLAB R2026a / Simulink. Saved batch comparison_20261014_224749_328, rerun with identical results on 16 October 2026.

Each trial simulates 10 s with a fault on link 1 from 2 s to 8 s. We score 450 samples per trial, 300 with the fault and 150 without. We ran 4, 6, 8 and 10 satellites, seven faults plus no fault, and five seeds: 160 trials per supervisor. 256 of them were new Simulink runs. The other 64 were saved V1 runs.

Detection means some alarm was raised during the fault. Exact isolation means the right fault was named. "Uncertain" counts as detection but not isolation, and it's never dropped from a score.

Recovery is the row that falls. V2 prevents five of V1's sensor-fault outages outright and creates no new ones. That leaves it the harder cases: power loss, blockage and the combined fault, which pointing can't fix. Perfect alignment gives 0.70 µW and 1.37 µW for the two power faults, and the receiver needs 1.5 µW.

The three optical faults all land at 76.3% for the same reason. V2 says "uncertain" while it nudges the beam to gather evidence, which takes about 1.4 s of the 6 s fault. Blockage stays at 0% on purpose, because a dark receiver can't tell a blockage from a dead transmitter. Sensor bias scores best because the separate rate sensor gives an independent check.

On the actuator faults V2 is worse than V1 on one measure. V1 had no false alarms there, mostly because it never noticed the faults at all. V2 does notice them, and the delay in estimating them causes 4.7% and 4.0% false alarms.

The first alarm came 0.02 s after the fault started. V2 then probed the beam for 1.4 s, estimated the offset and corrected it, and the link came back 2.58 s after it first dropped. At 8 s the fault cleared and the correction became wrong. V2 noticed, removed it and confirmed the link, but it was still down for 1.24 s after clearance. The direct link was down 3.82 s in all. Whenever it was down, traffic went via satellite 3, so the network never lost its route.

Harder conditions

We ran 24 paired trials with the fault moved to 2.7–6.7 s and angle noise raised to 0.2 µrad. They covered seeds 707 and 808, 4 and 10 satellites, and the pointing, sensor and combined faults.

V2 confirmed the link was back after the fault cleared in 12 of 12 runs. V1 did so in 4 of 12.

Nine automated checks also pass. For example, the supervisor never sees the true fault, dark links stay "uncertain", weak fits are rejected, and stale corrections are removed.

What this doesn't show

  • Anything about real hardware. This is a simplified simulation.
  • How to identify a fully dark link, or a sensor bias present from start-up.
  • How it behaves without accurate torque data, with several faulty links, with moving satellites or with real traffic.
  • That the methods are new. We haven't done the literature comparison to claim that.

All tables come from the CSVs in revisions/v2/results/. Every trial's raw data is in the repository.

Full results

From the MATLAB R2026a / Simulink comparison. Saved batch comparison_20261014_224749_328, rerun with identical results on 16 October 2026.

Each trial simulates 10 seconds. A fault is switched on for link 1 at 2 s and off at 8 s. Scoring starts at 1 s and uses one sample every 20 ms, so 450 samples per trial. That gives 300 samples with a fault and 150 without. We ran 4, 6, 8 and 10 satellites, seven faults plus a no-fault case, and five noise seeds (101, 202, 303, 404, 505). That's 160 trials for each supervisor. 64 of the V1 trials were reused from earlier saved runs. The other 256 were new Simulink runs.

Two definitions matter for reading everything below. Detection means the supervisor raised some alarm while a fault was active. Exact isolation means it named the correct fault. When the supervisor says "uncertain", that counts as detecting a fault but not as naming it. We never dropped uncertain answers from any score.

A standard deviation of 0.00 doesn't mean the system ignores noise. It means the discrete decisions came out the same for all five seeds at this display precision.

The recovery row is the one that goes down. V2 prevents five of V1's sensor-fault outages altogether, so they drop out of its count, and it didn't create any new outages. What's left for V2 includes power loss, blockage and the combined fault, and pointing can't fix any of those. With 82% power loss, perfect alignment still only gives 0.70 µW. With the combined fault, it gives 1.37 µW. The receiver needs 1.5 µW in both cases. We report the drop as it is, together with the counts.

The optical faults all score 76.3% exact isolation for the same reason. V2 says "uncertain" while it nudges the beam to collect evidence, and that takes about 1.4 s of the 6 s fault. Blockage stays at 0% on purpose. A dark receiver looks the same whether it's blocked or the transmitter is off, so V2 reports "uncertain". Sensor bias does best because the separate rate sensor gives a clear, independent check.

The actuator faults are where V2 is worse than V1 on one measure. V1 had 0% false alarms there, mostly because it didn't notice those faults at all. V2 does notice them, and the delay in estimating them produces 4.7% and 4.0% false alarms.

One trial, worked through

Saved trial n4_pointing_v2_seed101: four satellites, the beam misaligned by (23, 10) µrad between 2 s and 8 s, V2 supervisor.

Of the 300 fault samples, 229 were labelled "misaligned", 70 "uncertain" and 1 "healthy". Of the 150 healthy samples, 144 were labelled "healthy" and 6 "misaligned". So:

  • Detection accuracy = (299 + 144) / 450 = 98.44%
  • Exact isolation = 229 / 300 = 76.33%
  • False alarms = 6 / 150 = 4.00%
  • Uncertain = 70 / 450 = 15.56%

V2 raised its first alarm 0.02 s after the fault started. It then probed the beam for 1.4 s, estimated the offset and corrected it. The link came back 2.58 s after it first dropped. When the fault cleared at 8 s, the correction was now pointing the beam the wrong way. V2 noticed, removed it and confirmed the link again, but the link was still down for 1.24 s after clearance. The direct link was down 3.82 s in total. Whenever it was down, traffic went around it via satellite 3, so the network never lost its route.

Harder conditions

We also ran 24 paired trials with the fault moved to 2.7–6.7 s and angle noise raised to 0.2 µrad. These used seeds 707 and 808, 4 and 10 satellites, and the pointing, sensor and combined faults. V2 confirmed the link was back after the fault cleared in all 12 of its runs. V1 did so in 4 of 12.

Nine automated checks also pass. They cover things like: the supervisor never sees the true fault, a dark link stays "uncertain", fits with too little information are rejected, and old corrections get removed.

What these results don't show

  • Anything about real hardware. This is a simplified simulation.
  • Identifying the cause of a fully dark link, or a sensor bias present from start-up.
  • Performance without accurate torque measurements, with several faulty links at once, with moving satellites or with realistic traffic.
  • That these methods are new. We haven't done the literature comparison needed to claim that.
